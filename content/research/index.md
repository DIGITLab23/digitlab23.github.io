Research in the DIGIT Lab is driven by one overarching question: **How can AI augment our ability to design better materials, structures, and machines?** Our work focuses on two sides of AI-driven design.

First, we use AI to push the limits of what can be designed, centered on two challenges:

<div class="research-boxes">
  <div class="research-box">
    <h4>Complexity</h4>
    <p>Advances in modern manufacturing have unlocked unprecedented design freedom, but how can we effectively explore the resulting massive design space?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="research-box">
    <h4>Creativity</h4>
    <p>Human creativity is shaped and often limited by individual knowledge, experience, and cognitive biases, which makes innovation slow and unpredictable. Can modern AI help us discover high-performing, "outside-the-box" solutions that people might never consider?</p>
  </div>
</div>

Second, we want to keep humans at the center of the design process by addressing:

<div class="research-boxes">
  <div class="research-box">
    <h4>Trust</h4>
    <p>AI can generate design solutions, but when and why should we trust them?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="research-box">
    <h4>Knowledge</h4>
    <p>Beyond the solutions themselves, what can designers learn from AI (new design principles, insights, and intuition)?</p>
  </div>
</div>

We pursue these questions in the context of designing physical systems across diverse engineering domains.

For more information, you can explore our [**paper collection**](/publication/) or check out our [**open-source code**](https://www.github.com/DIGITLab23).
